## Exam pattern (consistent across all papers)

- **Total questions:** 8, attempt any 5
- **Marks:** 70, Time: 3 hours
- **Format:** bilingual (English + Hindi), each question typically split into (a) and (b) sub-parts worth equal marks
- Proof-based questions (induction, contradiction, uniqueness proofs) show up in nearly every paper — this subject rewards knowing standard proof templates cold, not just definitions

**Takeaway:** Groups (Unit 2), proof techniques + pigeonhole (Units 1 & 6), and graph theory basics (Unit 4) are the most consistently tested — appearing in nearly every paper. Rings and FSM appear rarely, so they're lower priority if you're short on revision time. Practice writing full proofs (not just stating theorems) since almost every paper has at least one "prove that..." question.

## December 2025

**Q1.** (a) Define countable and uncountable sets. Prove that the set of rational numbers is countable. (b) Using Venn diagram, prove: A∩(B∪C)=(A∩B)∪(A∩C).

**Q2.** (a) Define relation. Explain equivalence relation and partial ordering relation with suitable examples. (b) Let A={1,2,3}, R={(1,1),(2,2),(3,3),(1,2),(2,1)}. Check whether R is an equivalence relation and find equivalence classes.

**Q3.** (a) Define one-one, onto and bijective functions. Prove that inverse of a function exists if and only if the function is bijective. (b) State and prove Pigeonhole Principle.

**Q4.** (a) Construct truth table for the proposition (p→q)↔(¬p∨q) and show that it is a tautology. (b) Convert the following statement into predicate logic and write its negation: "Every student studies Discrete Mathematics."

**Q5.** (a) Prove by mathematical induction that: 1+2+3+…+n = n(n+1)/2. (b) Prove by contradiction that √2 is irrational.

**Q6.** (a) Define group and Abelian group. Prove that identity element of a group is unique. (b) Find all subgroups of the group Z₈.

**Q7.** (a) Define Finite State Machine. Explain FSM as a language recognizer. (b) Design a finite state machine that accepts all binary strings ending with 01.

**Q8.** (a) Define Euler path and Euler circuit. State necessary and sufficient conditions for their existence. (b) Explain Dijkstra's Algorithm and find the shortest path from a given source vertex in a weighted graph.

## June 2025

**Q1.** (a) Consider three sets A={1,2,3,4,5}, B={3,4,5,6}, C={1,5,7}. Using Venn diagrams, determine and shade the regions corresponding to (A∪B)∩(B∩C) and A∩(B∪C). Verify if these expressions are equivalent by listing the elements. (b) Define and illustrate an equivalence relation by constructing an equivalence relation on the set S={1,2,3,4,5,6} where two elements are related if they have the same remainder when divided by 3.

**Q2.** (a) What is pigeonhole principle? Prove it by using mathematical induction and use it to show that in a group of 15 people, at least two share the same birth month. (b) Explain a recursively defined function. Solve the recurrence relation f(n)=f(n−1)+3 with initial condition f(0)=2.

**Q3.** (a) Prove that the set G={0,1,2,3} under addition modulo 4 is an abelian group. Identify the identity and inverse elements. (b) Define a homomorphism between two groups. Verify whether the mapping f:ℝ→ℝ defined by f(x)=2x is a homomorphism under addition.

**Q4.** (a) Consider the propositions p, q and r, where p:"It is raining," q:"I am carrying an umbrella," r:"I stay dry." Assume the implication (p→q)→r. Construct a truth table for the expression (p→q)→r and determine if it is a tautology or contradiction. (b) What are predicates in propositional logic? Define universal and existential quantifiers with examples.

**Q5.** (a) Define graph theory and explain the basic terminology of graphs such as vertices, edges, degree and adjacency. (b) Define and give an example of an isomorphic graph pair. Verify isomorphism between two adjacency matrices.

**Q6.** (a) For the graph G with vertices V={A,B,C,D} and edges E={(A,B),(B,C),(C,D),(D,A),(A,C)}, determine: (i) Whether the graph contains a Eulerian path or circuit or not. (ii) All Hamiltonian circuits in the graph G.

**Q7.** (a) What is a Hasse diagram? Draw the Hasse diagram for the set {1,2,3,6,9,18} with the divisibility relation. (b) Define a lattice and prove that every finite lattice has a unique least upper bound and greatest lower bound.

**Q8.** Write short notes on any four: Finite state machines as language recognizers · Binomial theorem · Permutation group · Partial Ordering Relation · Countable and uncountable sets.

## December 2024

**Q1.** (a) Define various types of functions. How many symmetric and reflexive relations are possible from a set A containing 'n' elements? (b) Let Z be the group of integers with binary operation * defined by a*b = a+b−2, for all a,b∈Z. Find the identity element of the group ⟨Z,*⟩.

**Q2.** (a) Show that every Cyclic group is Abelian. Prove that a lattice with 5 elements is not a Boolean algebra. (b) Define Pigeon hole Principle. Write the contra positive of the implication: "if it is Sunday then it is a holiday."

**Q3.** (a) Show that there does not exist a graph with 5 vertices with degrees 1,3,4,2,3 respectively. (b) Obtain the generating function for the sequence 4,4,4,4,4,4. Explain complete digraph.

**Q4.** (a) Define planar graph. Prove that for any connected planar graph, v−e+r=2 where v,e,r is the number of vertices, edges, and regions of the graph respectively. (b) Prove that the relation R defined by "a is congruent to b modulo m" on the set of integers is an equivalence relation.

**Q5.** (a) A collection of 10 electric bulbs contain 3 defective ones. (i) In how many ways can a sample of four bulbs be selected? (ii) In how many ways can a sample of 4 bulbs be selected which contain 2 good bulbs and 2 defective ones? (iii) In how many ways can a sample of 4 bulbs be selected so that either the sample contains 3 good ones and 1 defective one or 1 good and 3 defective ones? (b) Discuss about Complete digraph and Euler Graph with the help of suitable examples.

**Q6.** (a) Solve the following recurrence equation using generating function: G(K) − 7G(K−1) + 10G(K−2) = 8K + 6. (b) Prove the validity of the following argument: "if the races are fixed so the casinos are crooked, then the tourist trade will decline. If the tourist trade decreases, then the police will be happy. The police force is never happy. Therefore, the races are not fixed."

**Q7.** (a) Let (L,∨,∧,≤) be a distributive lattice and a,b,c∈L. If a∧b=a∧c and a∨b=a∨c then show that b=c. (b) Explain various Rules of Inference for Propositional Logic.

**Q8.** (a) What is Ring? Define elementary properties of Ring with example. (b) Prove or disprove that intersection of two normal subgroups of a group G is again a normal subgroup of G.

## June 2024

**Q1.** (a) Explain the following: Euler Graph, Isomorphic graphs, Minimal spanning tree, Height of the tree. (b) Let Z be the group of integers with binary operation * defined by a*b=a+b−2, for all a,b∈Z. Find the identity element of the group ⟨Z,*⟩.

**Q2.** (a) Prove that the Complement of each element in a Boolean Algebra B is unique. (b) Let A be any finite set and P(A) be the power set of A⊆. Draw the Hasse diagrams of (P(A),⊆) for the following: (i) A={a} (ii) A={a,b} (iii) A={a,b,c} (iv) A={a,b,c,d}.

**Q3.** (a)(i) Prove that p∧q⇒q∨p is a Tautology. (ii) Show that (p∨q)∧(¬p)∧(¬q) is a contradiction. (b) Explain complete digraph and Euler Graph using suitable example of both.

**Q4.** (a) Define planar graph. Prove that for any connected planar graph, v−e+r=2 where v,e,r is the number of vertices, edges, and regions of the graph respectively. (b) Prove that the relation R defined by "a is congruent to b modulo m" on the set of integers is an equivalence relation.

**Q5.** (a) Prove that 5²ⁿ−1 is divisible by 24, where n is any positive integer. (b) Draw the Hasse diagram representing the positive divisors of 36 and 45.

**Q6.** (a) Show that the relation 'R' defined by (a,b) R (c,d) iff a+d=b+c is an equivalence relation. (b) Explain various Rules of Inference for Propositional Logic.

**Q7.** (a) Show that every Cyclic group is Abelian. Prove that a lattice with 5 elements is not a Boolean algebra. (b) Define Pigeon hole Principle. Write the contra positive of the implication: "if it is Sunday then it is a holiday."

**Q8.** (a) Prove that G={0,1,2,3,4,5,6} is an abelian group of order 7 with respect to addition modulo 7. (b) Prove or disprove that intersection of two normal subgroups of a group G is again a normal subgroup of G. Define subgroup, normal subgroup, Quotient group, with an example for each.
